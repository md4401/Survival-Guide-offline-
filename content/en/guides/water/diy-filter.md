title: How to make a homemade water filter
summary: Layered filter from a PET bottle. Always disinfect the water after filtering.
minutes: 30
difficulty: 1
warning: If the water smells strongly of fuel, sewage or chemicals, find another source. If anyone has diarrhoea, fever or vomiting after drinking suspect water, seek medical care.
now:
  - Let dirty water settle 1 to 2 hours. Pour off the top.
  - Cut the bottom off a 2-litre PET bottle. Pierce the cap.
  - Put clean cloth or a coffee filter in the neck.
  - Layer charcoal, fine sand, coarse sand, then gravel.
  - Rinse the filter with clean water until it runs clear. Discard.
  - Pour dirty water slowly. Collect the filtered water.
  - Disinfect: boil 1 minute, or 3 minutes above 2,000 m.
  - Store in a clean, covered container.
materials: [2 L PET bottle, Scissors or knife, Clean cloth or coffee filter, Wood charcoal (no additives), Fine sand (washed), Coarse sand (washed), Gravel, Clean container, Pot and fire, Chlorine tablets]
related: []
mistakes:
  - Assuming water is safe just because it looks clear.
  - Using barbecue charcoal with additives or briquettes.
  - Not washing the sand and charcoal first.
  - Using dirty containers and cloth.
  - Reversing the layers or pouring too fast.
  - Using the filter too long without replacing the layers.

- The filter removes dirt, leaves, turbidity and some larger parasites, but does not guarantee removal of bacteria, viruses or chemicals.
- That is why disinfection after filtering is a mandatory part of the process, not an extra.
- Commercial filters with 0.1 to 0.2 micron pores remove bacteria and protozoa, but viruses still need disinfection.
- Cloudy water also hampers chlorine and solar disinfection: filtering first improves it.
- Sewage, industrial, farm-runoff and sea water must not be treated with a homemade filter.
